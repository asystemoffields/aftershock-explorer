Reading an aftershock forecast

After a large earthquake, smaller (and occasionally larger) earthquakes follow. Their rate is remarkably regular: it decays roughly as 1/time (the modified Omori law), and the number scales with the mainshock size (Gutenberg-Richter). Combining these — the Reasenberg-Jones model — gives the chance of aftershocks of a given size over a given window.

  • This tool computes those chances live in your browser from published parameters and the mainshock magnitude. Pick a real earthquake or set a magnitude.
  • A forecast is not a prediction: it states probabilities, not what will happen.
  • The biggest aftershocks are the dangerous ones — note the chance of an aftershock larger than the mainshock (a sign the "mainshock" was a foreshock).
  • USGS issues authoritative operational forecasts for the US and territories; this is an open, global, educational complement, not a replacement.
